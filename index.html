The problem

National efforts are strong. Real gaps remain.

Saudi healthcare is shifting from treatment to prevention under Vision 2030. The missing link is targeted awareness that reaches every woman.

Gap 01

Little targeted prevention

Most initiatives focus on treatment rather than self-care and early prevention.

Gap 02

Stigma and embarrassment

Many girls and women hesitate to ask about reproductive and mental health because of shame or the lack of safe spaces.

Gap 03

Access and coverage

Lasting health messages and basic services rarely reach mobile communities and nomadic women in ways that respect their life and culture.

Mission & vision

Why Seha & Karama exists

Mission

Empower young women and women in Saudi Arabia with the knowledge and ability to practise self-care without embarrassment or stigma, through innovative and inclusive awareness tools for every part of society.

Vision

A health-aware society where every woman and girl, whatever her location or circumstances, has the knowledge to protect her physical and mental health with dignity and independence.

Operational blueprint

Three pillars that join the field and the digital

Hybrid education kits, live interactive workshops, and a network of ambassadors who carry awareness in their own community's language.

Pillar 01

Karama hybrid kit

Physical
A cloth bag of self-care essentials with visual flashcards built on pictograms for every literacy level.
Digital
A QR code opens multilingual audio and video resources, with downloadable content for offline access.
Pillar 02

Health impact ambassadors

On campus
Female students trained as Karama Ambassadors who spread awareness and run sessions in their colleges.
Nomadic communities
A trusted local champion from the community itself, speaking its language and breaking the stigma barrier.
Pillar 03

Safe dialogue spaces

Anonymous question box
Physical at workshops and digital through the QR code, for the most sensitive questions in full privacy.
Dialogue workshops
Supportive conversations, not dry academic lectures.
Inside the Karama Kit

Three corners in one bag

Tap a point on the bag to open each corner. Then try the live preview: scan the QR, play a sample, and flip the Sadu cards.

Corner A

Physical care essentials

Sustainable personal hygiene products for everyday dignity.

  • Menstrual hygiene supplies
  • Sanitizing wipes
  • Soothing skin moisturizer and gentle soap for sensitive skin
  • A small reusable warm compress to ease period cramps
Corner B

Sadu pictogram cards

Durable Sadu pictogram cards made from responsibly sourced, reusable or verified recycled-content materials, built on simple icons so they work at every literacy level.

  • Daily self-care and prevention steps
  • Normal symptoms vs. signs that need a check-up
  • Tracking the cycle, mood changes and mental health
Corner C

Safe digital bridge

An acrylic or fabric QR card built into the bag.

  • QR-enabled multilingual audio and video resources, with downloadable content for offline access
  • Voiced by ambassadors from the same local community
  • An anonymous question button with no personal data recorded

Every Karama Kit that reaches a Bedouin woman or a young university student is a window of knowledge and safety, and a step toward stronger preventive health.

How it works

The Karama journey, step by step

From the kit in her hands to support she can trust. Scroll to follow one beneficiary through it.

  1. STEP 01

    She receives the kit

    At a campus workshop or a field visit, she gets a Karama bag with self-care essentials and pictogram flashcards.

  2. STEP 02

    She scans the QR

    The code on the bag opens the library. One scan, no account, no personal data.

  3. STEP 03

    She listens offline

    Short episodes download once and play anywhere, even far from coverage.

  4. STEP 04

    She asks anonymously

    The hardest questions go into the anonymous box and get answered in the next session.

  5. STEP 05

    Support and measured impact

    When needed she is referred to a trusted partner, and pre and post surveys measure what changed.

Safe space

Ask without shame. We will never know who you are.

No name, number or email. Questions are collected and answered in general terms in upcoming workshops and episodes.

Preview version: the box connects to the question inbox at launch. In an emergency, contact your nearest health centre.

Is it normal to feel shy about asking?

Yes, and that is why this space exists. Asking about your health is your right and takes nothing from your dignity.

When should I see a specialist?

When something changes in your body or mood and it persists or affects your day. Through our partners we offer referrals to health associations and accredited clinics.

Does the content work for women who don't read?

Yes. The cards rely on pictograms, and most of the library is audio.

Project roadmap

Four phases, from preparation to measured impact

PHASE 01

Planning & preparation

Write the flashcard and audio content and assemble the kits.

PHASE 02

Training & partnerships

Recruit and train Karama Ambassadors and sign field partnerships.

PHASE 03

Field delivery

Distribute kits and run workshops and safe dialogue sessions.

PHASE 04

Impact & evaluation

Collect data and analyse awareness results among beneficiaries.

Impact measurement & evaluation

Measured from day one

Every workshop is assessed anonymously, so we know what changed without collecting personal data.

01

Pre- and post-workshop assessments

Short, anonymous QR-based questionnaires to measure improvements in participants’ health knowledge.

02

Three evaluation pillars

Prevention and self-care awareness, identification of early warning signs, and knowledge of professional support pathways.

03

Follow-up evaluation

Optional anonymous surveys conducted 4–6 weeks after workshops to assess self-reported behavior change.
